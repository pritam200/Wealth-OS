# Borrowing a regulated FIU to unlock India's Account Aggregator rails

An unregulated wealth-tracking app cannot be a Financial Information User (FIU) on India's Account Aggregator (AA) network in 2026, and no technology gateway changes that. The RBI (NBFC-AA) Directions, 2025 define an FIU as an entity registered with and regulated by a financial sector regulator ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12936)). The realistic routes are therefore (a) obtain a qualifying registration yourself (for a wealth tracker, most plausibly SEBI Registered Investment Adviser, which is a research inference, not sourced advice) or (b) run as a technology layer behind a regulated entity that is the FIU of record, using a Technology Service Provider (TSP) gateway for the integration. Of the vendors examined, **Setu is the best fit for engineering**: a self-serve sandbox, a multi-AA gateway, decrypted JSON and webhooks ([Setu docs](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/overview)). **Perfios FIU Lite is the only vendor with a published price** (Rs 1,000/month including 100 fetches, Rs 20 per extra fetch), but it is for regulated entities ([Perfios](https://perfios.ai/in/products/fiu-lite/)). Coverage is strong for singly held savings accounts, depository holdings and transactions (demat) and mutual-fund folios held at the registrars. It is partial for FDs/RDs (about 40% of banks) and absent for credit cards, joint and NRE/NRO accounts, and EPF/PPF. Continuous sync means a PERIODIC consent plus FIU-scheduled fetches. There is no push of new transactions. The recommendation is to build and test the adapter against sandboxes now, keep CAS/statement import for credit cards and gaps, and settle the legal "FIU of record" question before any production go-live. That question is the critical-path item, not the code.

## The regulatory gate decides everything, and no gateway removes it

The RBI Directions effective 28 Nov 2025 replaced the 2016 Master Direction. They regulate the AA itself and define FIU and FIP as roles ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12936); [Treelife](https://treelife.in/fintech/nbfc-account-aggregator-licence-eligibility/)). Sahamati states that only entities registered with RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA may be FIPs and FIUs ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/how-to-join-the-account-aggregator-network-to-share-and-access-financial-data/)). Its registry lists the permitted FIU classes: stock brokers, registered investment advisers, portfolio managers, research analysts, NBFCs, banks, insurers and brokers, mutual funds, AIFs, payment aggregators and pension points of presence ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/fip-fiu-in-account-aggregators-ecosystem/)). Central Registry onboarding treats a regulator-issued Certificate of Registration as the mandatory first document ([Sahamati Member Success](https://membersuccess.sahamati.org.in/llms-full.txt)). Setu's own licences page says there is no pathway for unregulated entities ([Setu](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/licenses-and-go-live/licenses)). The research found no 2024-2026 change opening FIU status to unregulated entities; that is absence of evidence, not proof.

A TSP is the one unregulated party in the AA data flow. It supplies pre-built FIU/FIP modules and speeds certification, but the regulated entity is named in the Central Registry and remains the FIU of record ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/how-to-join-the-account-aggregator-network-to-share-and-access-financial-data/); [Sahamati Member Success](https://membersuccess.sahamati.org.in/llms-full.txt)). Setu's go-live flow has it absorb certification by a Sahamati-empanelled auditor (Aujas) on the FIU's behalf ([Setu](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/licenses-and-go-live/go-live)). Nobody found documents a "reseller FIU" model that lets an unregulated app consume data under a TSP's cover, so an unregulated app downstream of a regulated FIU is, legally, a contractual sub-recipient. Whether that satisfies the AA's recipient-identity and purpose-limit rules is unconfirmed and needs counsel plus a written answer from the chosen AA. The Sahamati Code of Conduct v2.1 (dated 21 Jul 2026), which holds the FIU sub-delegation and third-party-sharing rules, was not read and is the most important unread document.

There is a second, product-level gate. Sahamati's personal finance management consent template CT008 limits eligible FIUs to regulated entities with an advisory or distribution charter and no fiduciary relationship ([Sahamati CT008](https://sahamati.org.in/aa-consent-template-library/consent-template-id-ct008-personal-finance-management/)). It allows periodic fetch, up to 45 successful fetches a month, a consent validity of at most 1 year, and a data range of 10 years for SEBI-regulated types and 13 months for others. So even a regulated partner must fit that charter for the template that makes daily refresh legal. Setu's 2023 guide names a SEBI Registered Investment Adviser (45-90 days, Rs 2,000 fee) as a lighter route to eligibility ([Setu](https://setu.co/blog/licences-for-fintechs-to-go-live-on-account-aggregator)). Those figures are three years old and SEBI's RIA norms have since been revised, so treat them as unverified. RIA status also brings advisory-conduct obligations that a pure tracker may not want.

Sahamati's role is operational. It runs the Central Registry and token services (free of charge), and membership is priced separately on request ([Sahamati Member Success](https://membersuccess.sahamati.org.in/llms-full.txt)). RBI recognised it as the self-regulatory organisation on 5 Jun 2026 ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/media-article/rbi-sro-recognition-account-aggregator-ecosystem/)). Registry onboarding needs two or more named contacts across five roles, a customer-experience and a legal-adherence checklist, and a customer-journey demo. The minimum processing time is 3 working days for a complete submission. A "Revised CR Onboarding Process" took effect 20 Apr 2026, but its contents were not readable.

DPDP does not change the gate but shapes the build. The Rules were notified 13 Nov 2025. Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) opens around Nov 2026, and the substantive duties on notice, consent, security, breach reporting and erasure apply from about 13 May 2027 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)). The AA's own prohibition on storing data and on using it beyond the consent artefact binds the AA, not the FIU ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12936)). The FIU-side equivalents (use only for the consented purpose, delete at data-life expiry) are expected from the Code of Conduct but unverified. Design now for the stricter of the two regimes: store your own consent record, delete on revocation or expiry, and keep processing logs.

## The shortlist: Setu leads on engineering, Perfios on price transparency, Finvu and CAMS on data depth

All shortlisted routes ultimately sit on the same ReBIT specifications and the Sahamati registry, so any FIU can in principle reach any FIP through any AA. In practice each FIP is connected to only some AAs. The backbone entities (CDSL, NSDL, CAMS, KFin, GSTN) are each on 13 AAs, but banks and insurers are uneven ([Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/account-aggregator-integration-fintech-builders-2026/)). PhonePe's exit in Feb 2025, with about 50 million registered users but only about 20 live FIPs against 58 at CAMSfinserv, shows that FIP depth beats user count ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-phonepe-exits-account-aggregator-business-cites-onboarding-challenges/)). Live-FIP counts per a competitor-authored guide are Anumati 80+, CAMS 70+, OneMoney 65+, Finvu 60+ and NADL 60+ ([HyperVerge](https://hyperverge.co/blog/best-account-aggregators/)). This means a single-AA integration leaves coverage holes, and a multi-AA gateway is the usual remedy.

Reliability is the other half of coverage. The only quantitative linking figures are from FY24: success for new-to-AA customers of 35.8% rising to 53.3%, and for existing AA users 57% rising to 74.4%, with a "good" data-fetch failure rate below 5% ([Sahamati case study summary](https://sahamati.org.in/fip-discovery-on-aa-performance-case-study/)). No 2026 ecosystem fetch success rate, per-AA uptime log or bank-wise league table is public. ## Continuous sync is periodic consent plus your own scheduler, with no push of new transactions

A consent request is a signed JSON object with consent start and expiry, DataLife, FI types, purpose, fetchType ONETIME or PERIODIC, Frequency, FIDataRange and consentMode. The user approves it on the AA's screen after a redirect, and the AA issues a digitally signed artefact ([Setu consent flow](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/v1/api-integration/consent-flow); [Setu consent object](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/consent-object)). Consent statuses are PENDING, ACTIVE, PAUSED, REVOKED, EXPIRED and REJECTED. The raw ReBIT endpoints, with medium confidence, are POST /Consent, GET /Consent/handle/{h}, POST /FI/request, GET /FI/fetch/{sessionId}, and AA-to-FIU callbacks at /Consent/Notification and /FI/Notification. Every message carries a detached RS256 JWS in the `x-jws-signature` header ([Sahamati router spec](https://developer.sahamati.org.in/sahamatinet-poc/integration-steps/integration-with-router/router-apis-specifications/fiu-api-specification)). FI data is encrypted with ephemeral Curve25519 ECDH keys, then HKDF and AES-GCM. Notifications are event-driven only (CONSENT_STATUS_UPDATE, SESSION_STATUS_UPDATE), so there is no webhook for "new transaction" ([Setu notifications](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/api-integration/notifications)).

Continuity therefore comes from a periodic consent. For PFM, one consent per user should use PERIODIC fetch, STORE mode, wealth-management purpose code 101 or 102, and PROFILE, SUMMARY and TRANSACTIONS. The schema caps frequency at one request per hour and 24 per day ([Setu consent object](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/consent-object)), but CT008 is much tighter at 45 fetches a month and one-year validity. A daily cron per consent therefore fits comfortably. Each fetch creates a new session. Moneyone's gateway fetches incrementally if no date range is given on a periodic request, and Setu offers Auto-Fetch ([Moneyone FI request](https://developer.moneyone.in/api-reference/data/fi-request.md); [Setu data APIs](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/api-integration/data-apis)). The raw ReBIT API has no incremental flag, so that behaviour is gateway-specific. Re-consent is needed at least annually, so alert users about 30 days before expiry. Data freshness is the FIP's state at request time, and staleness of 1-2 days is reported by wealth apps ([Fold docs](https://docs.fold.money/introduction-to-aa)).

For the existing adapter interface, the design inferences are as follows. They are the researchers' suggestions, not specification. Keep a per-(consent, account) watermark and request a window from the last successful end date minus a 2-3 day overlap. Upsert transactions by account, transaction id, value date, amount and narration hash, because some banks omit ids or timestamps ([Tech Advisory](https://techadvisory.substack.com/p/account-aggregator-current-challenges)). Treat mutual-fund, equity, NPS and deposit summaries as snapshots and take the latest rather than diffing. Reconcile by polling for missed webhooks, since Setu webhooks do not retry and no signature verification is documented. Version the FI parsers, because schemas have changed since 2022.

For Java, no official vendor SDK was found for Setu, Moneyone (listed "Coming Soon") or Finvu. With a gateway, a plain Spring `RestClient` or `WebClient` suffices, because the gateway returns decrypted JSON and manages keys. With a raw integration you assemble three open-source or built-in pieces: Sahamati's Rahasya Java service for Curve25519 ([Sahamati/rahasya](https://github.com/Sahamati/rahasya)), Finvu's Java detached-JWS reference, and the JDK's built-in X25519 key agreement with BouncyCastle for HKDF. No complete open-source Java FIU client exists, and the exact HKDF parameters should be copied from the Rahasya source rather than assumed. Choose the gateway route, because raw integration also needs Central Registry onboarding, which requires a regulated entity.

## A phased plan that ships value before the licence question is settled

Phase 0 (now, no regulatory dependency): keep the existing CAS import and forwarding inbox as the production source for mutual funds and demat, and add statement upload for credit cards and FD/RD gaps. These need no AA at all. Credit cards will remain on this path whichever vendor is chosen.

Phase 1 (weeks 1-4): implement the provider adapter against the Setu sandbox (self-serve, mock FIPs for multiple FI types) and optionally the Finvu and Sahamati sandboxes. Build the consent lifecycle table, signed webhook controller, per-account watermark, scheduled periodic fetch, expiry alerts and DPDP-style consent and deletion handling. Sandbox work needs no licence, so this phase de-risks the code in parallel with legal work.

Phase 2 (in parallel, the long pole): decide the FIU-of-record route. Option A is a regulated partner (an RIA, broker, distributor or NBFC) that takes FIU responsibility under a written agreement. Option B is your own SEBI RIA or similar registration, with the 45-90 day figure unverified and the advisory-conduct burden acknowledged. In either case, get written answers from the chosen TSP and AA on three questions. Can an unregulated product layer receive the data? Is purpose code 101/102 or CT008 available to the FIU class? What does the Code of Conduct say about sub-delegation and deletion?

Phase 3 (production on the first AA path): go live through Setu, or through Perfios FIU Lite if the regulated partner prefers a published price, with the partner as FIU of record. Request CAS-backed demat and MF first, then savings, then FD/RD as best effort. Show per-institution status and fall back to upload on failure.

Phase 4 (hardening): add a second AA path (Finvu or CAMSfinserv for MF depth, or rely on Setu's multi-AA routing), consult the FIP Health API before fetches, and track per-FIP success. Revisit credit cards, EPF and joint accounts each quarter against Sahamati's FI-type list.

## Where the sources disagree

The notes disagree in several places, and none are resolved here.

**Network statistics.** Live FIPs are 177 in Jan 2026, 179 at 31 Mar 2026, 176 at the Jun 2026 SRO announcement, and 176 on an undated HyperVerge page ([Sahamati Varta Jan 2026](https://sahamati.org.in/varta-the-sahamati-newsletter-january-2026/); [Sahamati SRO release](https://sahamati.org.in/media-article/rbi-sro-recognition-account-aggregator-ecosystem/)). FIUs are 913 (Jan 2026), 989 (31 Mar 2026), 1,020 (SRO release) and 1,076 (HyperVerge, undated), a monotonic progression that may simply reflect dates. The AA count is 17 operating in Jan 2026, but Medianama counted 16 after PhonePe's Feb 2025 exit, and the notes could not identify the replacement. Cumulative fulfilled consents are 390M+ in Jan 2026, 45 crore (450M) at 31 Mar 2026, and 566.26M at 31 Aug 2026 on the live dashboard, but one Sahamati snippet reports 500M "as of 2 Sept 2026". That conflicts with the dashboard and was not reconciled ([Sahamati dashboard](https://sahamati.org.in/aa-dashboard/)). Linked accounts range from 263M (Jan 2026) to 284.6M (Mar), 310M+ (later snippet) and 338.04M cumulative (Aug dashboard). The dashboard is the best primary source, and the conflicting snippets should be treated as lower-confidence.

**Credit cards.** No source says cards are live, and none says they are not. The absence rests on the 16-type list of Sep 2025, which is now more than a year old, plus two March 2026 articles that do not mention cards. Setu claims 23 FI types and Sahamati's FI-types page was updated in 2026, so a card schema could exist without being in the Sep 2025 list. The researchers assumed "not available"; confirm directly.

**CAMS and KFintech transactions.** One note says it could not confirm whether the registrar FIPs return transactions or only holdings. The other notes list SIPs and physical MF units under the registrars and describe MF as a live FI type, and the PFM template CT008 lists transaction data. CAMSfinserv's registrar feed is described as collected once a day. A further conflict is that a March 2026 vendor blog says CAMS and KFin are live with 13 AAs for MF folios, while the Sahamati advisory says demat folios were disabled at the registrars from Nov 2025 (KFin) and 1 Mar 2026 (CAMS). The two are reconcilable if the blog refers only to non-demat folios, but the notes do not say so.

**Other conflicts.** Depository history is "capped at 2 years" in a vendor blog, while CT008 allows a 10-year range for SEBI-regulated types; the template maximum may not be what depositories actually serve. Insurance policies and ULIPs are "withdrawn" on a Dec 2024 Sahamati page but live in the Sep 2025 deck. The purpose-code table differs between two Setu pages (code 101 appears as both "wealth management" and "loan underwriting"); Sahamati's list says 101 is wealth management ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/purpose-codes/)). Finvu pricing is quoted as per-account and per-fetch rates in one place and as custom-only in another page of the same directory. Finvu's founding year is 2017 or 2019 depending on the source. Whether a regulated FIU must also register as an FIP is stated as reciprocity by some sources and as a Nov 2023 requirement by one weak source.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. Choosing a vendor is the easy part, since Setu, Perfios, Moneyone, Finvu and CAMSfinserv are all technically sufficient for bank, demat and mutual-fund data. The binding constraint is that the legal FIU must be a regulated entity, and the consent template that permits daily PFM refresh further assumes an advisory or distribution charter. This means the commercial and compliance work (finding or becoming that entity) should start now, while the adapter is built against the self-serve sandboxes.

The coverage picture also argues against making AA the sole source. Credit cards, joint and NRE/NRO accounts, EPF/PPF and most FDs and RDs stay outside it, the depository history is limited, and journey success is plausibly well short of 100%. The CAS and statement-import paths already in the product are therefore a permanent complement, not a stopgap, and the AA integration is best framed as an automatic-refresh layer on top of them.
